## Where things go

| Folder | What lives here |
| --- | --- |
| `Journal/` | One note per working day. Created for you by the calendar icon. |
| `Projects/` | Notes about a *thing* rather than a *day*: your network plan, your domain, your progress. Edited over months. |
| `Resources/` | Reference material you did not write for one particular day: cheatsheets, scripts, reports. |
| `Templates/` | The skeleton new daily notes are stamped from. You will rarely open it. |

The split that matters is the first two. A daily note answers "what
happened on Tuesday". A project note answers "what is my network
addressing". Six months from now you will want the second kind, and you
will not remember which Tuesday to look under.

## The four headings

Every daily note arrives with the same four, and the second one is the
reason this works.

> [!quote] What I did
> The easy one. What you built, in what order, and what took longest.

> [!bug] What broke (and how it ended)
> Everybody records their successes. Almost nobody records the twenty
> minutes lost to a typo in a config file, which is the note that saves
> you the next time you make it. Write the wrong guess as well as the
> right answer.

> [!abstract] What I learned
> One thing, in your own words. Explaining it to the page is how you find
> out whether you actually understood it.

> [!question] Open questions
> Anything that still feels like magic. These are checkboxes on purpose.
> Crossing one off in three months is the most satisfying thing in here.

## The habit

> [!warning] Commit at the end of every session
> Even a short one. It takes ten seconds and it buys you a point you can
> return to. `git add -A`, then `git commit -m "..."`, then `git push`.
> Lesson 1.3 sets this up and [[git-basics]] has the rest.

## Making it yours

This is a starting shape, not a rulebook. Add folders, rename things,
throw out the parts you do not use. The only pieces the course actually
depends on are `Journal/`, `Projects/lab-progress.md`, and
`Resources/scripts/`.
